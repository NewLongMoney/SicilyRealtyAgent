Why Nairobi's Prime Residential Market Held Firm in Q1 2026

While global property markets softened in early 2026, Nairobi's premium residential corridor — Westlands to Kilimani — recorded a 12% year-on-year uplift in transacted values. The driver is structural: supply of grade-A residential units remains constrained while demand from diaspora buyers, returning professionals, and domestic HNW individuals continues to grow. The Konza corridor adds another layer — satellite demand is now measurably pulling buyers further from the CBD than at any point in the last decade.

The Roads Being Built Right Now That Will Reprice Nairobi Property

Infrastructure is the oldest signal in real estate. When a road cuts travel time by half, the land around it reprices — sometimes before the tarmac is dry. Phase 2 of the Nairobi Expressway, now operational, has done exactly that for Runda and Gigiri. The Railway City project — 60% complete and tracking a 2027 handover — is already moving values in its catchment zone upward by 18%. Buyers paying attention to infrastructure are buying 18 months ahead of the market. Buyers ignoring it are paying tomorrow's price today.
